# back-exo-biomechanics
This project investigates how a passive back exoskeleton can reduce spinal loading during lifting, work carried out as part of my PhD in Technology for Health. The aim was to quantify, in an objective and physiologically grounded way, whether such a device actually protects the lower back during physically demanding tasks.
The device studied is the MATE-XB (Comau, Italy), a passive trunk-support exoskeleton. It stores energy as the user bends forward and releases it as assistance during the lift, helping the back muscles without motors or external power. Evaluating its effect is challenging, because the forces acting inside the spine cannot be measured directly. To overcome this, I combined experimental measurements — motion capture and surface electromyography (EMG) — with subject-specific musculoskeletal modeling using OpenSim and the CEINMS toolbox. This EMG-driven approach estimates individual muscle forces and the compression force at the L5-S1 joint, the most loaded region of the lower spine.
The project includes two complementary studies. The first was conducted under controlled laboratory conditions, with participants performing standardised symmetrical and asymmetrical lifts. It showed that the exoskeleton reduced lumbar loading and back muscle activity while preserving natural movement. The second was a preliminary study under semi-realistic, industry-like conditions, where participants repeatedly lifted, carried, and repositioned a 10 kg box. It confirmed reductions in lumbar extensor moments and muscle activity, and demonstrated that the modeling pipeline works in conditions closer to real workplace demands.
Together, these studies show that EMG-driven musculoskeletal modeling is a reliable tool for assessing occupational exoskeletons, and that the MATE-XB offers measurable biomechanical benefits for lifting tasks. The work contributes to the development of ergonomic interventions aimed at preventing work-related musculoskeletal disorders, which remain a leading cause of disability worldwide.
